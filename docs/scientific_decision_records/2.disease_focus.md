## Context
Disease model and data modality choices
Why these biomarkers? Why these assay types? What scientific hypotheses does the platform exist to test, and what data would falsify them? This is the scientific equivalent of the platform decision — it constrains everything about what data products matter.

The translational hypothesis
What is the assumed chain from molecular target → disease mechanism → clinical outcome → measurable endpoint? This is the most important scientific decision in a biotech and it's almost never written down explicitly. An ADR that captures it — including what would cause it to be revised — is genuinely valuable.

Data quality thresholds for decision-making
At what data quality level is a Discovery finding considered strong enough to advance to Translational Medicine? What quality threshold separates exploratory from submission-bound? You've implemented a governance tier system in ADR-4 — the scientific ADR should explain the scientific rationale for where those thresholds sit.

External data integration assumptions
Which public databases are trusted sources (ChEMBL, UniProt, ClinVar, etc.), at what version, and what are their known limitations for your specific use case? This maps directly to your known_limitations and provenance.source_version_or_date fields in ADR-4 — the scientific ADR justifies what those fields should contain.

The reproducibility standard
What does it mean for a scientific result to be reproducible on this platform? Is it computational reproducibility (same code, same data, same result) or biological reproducibility (independent experiment, same conclusion)? This is often assumed and never stated, which creates silent disagreements between Research and Regulatory Affairs later.


The recommendation: Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy (DMD)
DMD scores on every criterion and is better than any alternative I evaluated.
Criterion 1 — Synbio adjacency: strong. DMD is a monogenic disorder caused by dystrophin gene mutations. The therapeutic landscape is entirely molecular — exon skipping, microdystrophin gene therapy, CRISPR-based approaches, RNA modulation. FDA-approved therapies include RNA-based approaches and microdystrophin gene therapy, directly in your domain. PubMed Central You can discuss the biology authentically, not performatively.
Criterion 2 — Gene therapy pipeline: exceptional. The trial activity in 2025 alone includes multiple Phase 2 and Phase 3 gene therapy trials from Sarepta, PepGen, Precision Biosciences, Wave Life Sciences, and others — with new data readouts every month. GlobalData This is one of the most active gene therapy disease areas in existence right now. The commercial pressure to get data infrastructure right is intense and immediate.
Criterion 3 — Public data: unusually rich for a rare disease. This is DMD's single greatest advantage over alternatives. The Duchenne Regulatory Science Consortium (D-RSC) database includes data from DMD clinical trials, natural history studies, and clinical data collections, and is open to non-consortium members upon data use committee approval. C-Path The RDCA-DAP platform has DMD datasets. CDISC has published a DMD Therapeutic Area User Guide including a cardiac imaging supplement. A landmark 2025 Nature Communications study analysed over 700 longitudinal serum samples and identified prognostic protein biomarkers PharmiWeb — publicly available data from a large, well-characterised cohort.
Criterion 4 — Scientifically contested data model: very strong. DMD has multiple live endpoint debates that make for genuinely interesting data product problems. The primary endpoint question — functional motor tests vs. digital biomarkers vs. dystrophin levels vs. imaging — is actively contested. The stride velocity 95th centile has been validated as a robust endpoint approved by EMA, but integrating digital outcome measures with traditional measures in a unified data model remains an unsolved problem. PubMed The biomarker standardisation work is ongoing. The identification of specific, accurate, sensitive biomarkers is becoming crucial, but translating research results to clinical practice faces significant challenges and bottlenecks. PubMed That's where a well-built data mesh makes a visible difference.
Criterion 5 — Community and industry engagement: exceptional. Parent Project Muscular Dystrophy (PPMD) is one of the most sophisticated patient advocacy organisations in rare disease. The D-RSC consortium brings together FDA, pharma sponsors, and academic researchers around shared data standards. Multiple companies are actively running trials. The community will notice a well-built public data mesh and will engage with it.
Criterion 6 — Tractability: strong. You can start with the D-RSC database (request access), the RDCA-DAP DMD datasets, ClinicalTrials.gov trial-level data, and the published biomarker literature. That's enough to build a meaningful first version without proprietary data. The CDISC DMD TAUG gives you the schema starting point — you're not inventing the data model from scratch.

Why not the obvious alternatives
CAR-T / general oncology: Too crowded. Every pharma data consultant claims oncology expertise. The data products that exist are already sophisticated. You'd be one of many rather than one of few.
Sickle cell disease: Strong gene therapy pipeline, good public data. Loses on synbio adjacency — the biology is haematological rather than molecular-genetic in the way that maps to synbio thinking. Also loses on data model complexity — the primary endpoint question is relatively settled.
Spinal muscular atrophy (SMA): Very close second. Strong on criteria 1–5. Loses narrowly on criterion 3 — the public data infrastructure is less developed than DMD, and the primary therapeutic modality (Spinraza/Zolgensma) is already commercially mature, reducing the urgency of new data infrastructure.
Huntington's disease: Strong public data (PREDICT-HD, EnrollHD), active pipeline, contested biomarkers. Loses on synbio adjacency — it's a CNS disease where the relevant expertise is neuroscience, not molecular biology. You'd be learning from scratch rather than extending.

What to build first
The most visible and useful first data product for DMD is a unified natural history + biomarker data model on Databricks that joins the D-RSC clinical trial data to the published biomarker literature with a consistent schema. The specific problem to solve: the endpoint data and the biomarker data currently live in separate worlds, making it hard to evaluate whether a biomarker actually predicts functional outcome in the real trial data. That's the gap. Building a data mesh that closes it, with a clean schema that reflects the biology rather than the source system, is exactly the kind of scientific data product that will get noticed by the D-RSC community, by pharma data leads running DMD trials, and by the biotech startups in the space.

Scientific ADRs — DMD Data Mesh Platform
Written from the client's (programme team's) perspective

ADR-S1: Which disease is targeted and why
Decision
Target Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy (DMD), specifically the exon-skipping eligible subpopulations, as the first disease instantiation of the platform.
Rationale
DMD is not selected because it is common — it affects approximately 1 in 5,000 male births. It is selected because the structure of the disease makes the data problem unusually tractable and the data infrastructure unusually valuable simultaneously. Three properties of DMD make it the correct beachhead.
Property 1: Therapy eligibility is computable from mutation data. The reading frame rule, characterised by out-of-frame inactivating DMD gene mutations in severe DMD and in-frame mutations in milder Becker muscular dystrophy, is linked with protein results in around 75% of cases. PubMed Central This means that for the majority of patients, whether a given exon-skipping therapy is biologically applicable can be determined algorithmically from the mutation record — specifically from the exon address, the resulting reading frame shift, and the predicted restoration. Single and double exon-skipping approaches are applicable for 92.8% of large deletions and 90.3% of all mutations analysed. Exon 51 is the most relevant single target, applicable for 10.6% of all mutations and 17.2% of large deletions. PubMed Central A platform that encodes this logic as a transformation — not a lookup table, but a rule applied at the data layer — immediately converts a mutation record into an eligibility classification. No other disease in the neuromuscular space has this property to the same degree.
Property 2: The registry infrastructure already exists at scale. TREAT-NMD registries encompass over 13,500 DMD patients across 31 countries, with 94% usage reported for clinical research. Academia.edu This is not a data collection problem — it is a data integration and semantic modelling problem. The raw material exists. The platform's value is in connecting it to trial eligibility criteria, natural history trajectories, and endpoint definitions in a scientifically valid schema. Most rare diseases do not have this registry infrastructure; DMD is exceptional.
Property 3: The regulatory forcing events have created buyer urgency. Real-world data from patient registries failed to provide conclusive evidence due to methodological limitations and biases, and the EMA concluded in 2024 that the effectiveness of Translarna could not be confirmed, resulting in non-renewal of its conditional marketing authorisation in March 2025. PubMed Central The field has a recent, visible, expensive example of what happens when registry data cannot speak to clinical outcomes in a mutation-stratified way. Every programme team in DMD has read this. The platform directly addresses the mechanism of that failure.
Why not other diseases first
SMA is the closest alternative. It also has mutation-specific therapies and registry infrastructure. The argument against starting there: SMN1 copy number is the primary stratification variable in SMA, and it is already more standardised across registries than DMD's exon-level mutation data. The data problem is less severe, so the platform's marginal value is lower. DMD's mutation heterogeneity and the active endpoint controversy make it the higher-value starting point.

ADR-S2: Which biomarkers and assay types, and why
Decision
The platform integrates four biomarker tiers in order of regulatory maturity: (1) functional endpoints as the primary clinical anchor, (2) serum protein biomarkers as pharmacodynamic monitors, (3) quantitative MRI as objective disease progression markers, and (4) dystrophin expression as the molecular surrogate.
This is not a choice of one over the others — it is a decision about which biomarkers to model in the platform, in what relationship to each other, and with what metadata.
Tier 1: Functional endpoints — the clinical anchor
Functional tests such as the 6-minute walk test (6MWT), North Star Ambulatory Assessment (NSAA), 10-metre walk-run test velocity, and Performance of Upper Limb (PUL 2.0) are used to evaluate disease progression in routine clinical visits and serve as clinical trial endpoints. However, these tests are often affected by patient motivation, evaluator engagement, and have high intra-patient variability, limiting the possibility to detect relevant changes in patients' trajectories. medRxiv
The platform must encode these endpoints with full context: age at assessment, ambulatory status, corticosteroid exposure, and mutation class. Without these covariates, NSAA scores from different registries are not comparable — the same score means different things at age 6 versus age 10, in a steroid-naïve versus steroid-treated patient, and in a patient with an exon 51-skippable deletion versus a nonsense mutation patient. The platform's schema encodes this context as structured fields, not as free-text notes.
Tier 2: Serum protein biomarkers — pharmacodynamic monitoring
A large-scale serum proteomics study analysing 702 serum samples from 153 male DMD patients found 318 aptamers corresponding to 294 proteins significantly associated with motor performance across two sites, with most associations found with lower limb functional tests. Thirty-six proteins were associated with disease milestones including RGMA, ART3, ANTXR2, and DLK1, showing promise as prognostic biomarkers for patient stratification and monitoring of interventions. Nature
The platform models the biomarkers with the strongest validated associations: creatine kinase (CK) as the marker of muscle membrane damage; creatine/creatinine ratio (Cr/Crn) and myostatin as longitudinal functional correlates. Longitudinal observations of myostatin and the creatine/creatinine ratio are associated with functional tests including the 6MWT, NSAA, 10-metre walk-run test velocity, and PUL 2.0, as well as disease milestones including loss of ambulation. medRxiv These are blood-based, non-invasive, and scalable across registry sites — which makes them practical anchors for external control arm construction.
The assay type matters here: Cr/Crn is measured by liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS); myostatin by ELISA; CK by standard clinical chemistry. These have different inter-laboratory variability profiles, and the platform must capture assay method as a structured metadata field, not assume homogeneity across sites.
Tier 3: Quantitative MRI — objective progression marker
MR spectroscopy fat fraction (FF) and MRI transverse relaxation time (MRI-T2), performed in multiple leg muscles, show high reproducibility with Pearson's R greater than 0.95. Responsiveness to disease progression varies by disease stage across muscles. PubMed Central Quantitative MRI measures are noninvasive and objective, and if standardised approaches are carefully implemented, provide highly reproducible results across sites and day-to-day in DMD. PubMed Central
The platform stores MRI-derived fat fraction and T2 values with full acquisition metadata: muscle group, MRI vendor, field strength, acquisition sequence. These variables affect absolute values and must be treated as potential confounders in any cross-site analysis. Dixon fat fraction is the most widely collected; MRS-T2 is more sensitive but less widely available. Both are modelled.
Tier 4: Dystrophin expression — molecular surrogate
Elevidys received accelerated approval in 2023 based on levels of dystrophin as a surrogate biomarker, with the FDA concluding that an increase in the surrogate endpoint — expression of microdystrophin — is reasonably likely to predict clinical benefit. PubMed Central However, Study 301 failed to demonstrate a statistically significant difference between treatment and placebo in the NSAA primary outcome, casting doubt on the validity of micro-dystrophin expression as a surrogate endpoint predictive of clinical improvement. Taylor & Francis Online
The platform stores dystrophin expression data — percentage of normal by Western blot or capillary Western; by muscle (tibialis anterior is most common); by antibody used. The controversy around this surrogate is precisely why the platform's value lies in being able to correlate dystrophin expression data with functional trajectories across patients and studies. The platform does not resolve the controversy — it makes it answerable with data.

ADR-S3: The scientific hypothesis the platform tests, and what would falsify it
The platform hypothesis

The combination of mutation class, age-at-assessment, corticosteroid exposure, ambulatory status, and serum biomarker trajectory — encoded as a structured, queryable dataset — produces a natural history model that is more predictive of functional decline and treatment response than any single variable or unstructured dataset alone.

This is not a drug hypothesis. It is a data science hypothesis about the predictive value of a correctly structured, multi-source dataset for DMD programme planning.
What the hypothesis predicts
If correct, the platform should enable:

External control arms for single-arm trials that are statistically credible when matched on mutation class, age, corticosteroid status, and baseline functional score — because these variables together explain a large fraction of outcome variance
Prospective identification of the patient subgroup most likely to show a detectable treatment effect within a 52-week trial window — because the natural history trajectory is predictable from baseline biomarker profile
Endpoint selection guidance — specifically, identification of which functional test is most sensitive at which disease stage and in which mutation subgroup

What would falsify it
The hypothesis fails under three specific conditions:
Falsification 1: Mutation class does not substantially explain variance in functional trajectory after adjusting for age and steroid exposure. This would mean the reading frame rule, while biologically true, does not produce clinically meaningful differences in disease rate that are detectable within trial timeframes. A study of DMD gene variant classes found they are a relatively minor cause of motor outcome variability, suggesting sensitivity or subgroup analyses based on variant classes are not warranted in typical clinical trial statistical analysis plans. Neurology This is a genuine partial falsification signal — not definitive, but it means the platform's mutation-stratification layer needs to be validated against functional data, not assumed.
Falsification 2: Cross-site biomarker data is not harmonisable to the level required for external control arm construction. If the variability introduced by different assay methods, clinical sites, and patient selection criteria exceeds the effect sizes expected from treatment, the platform cannot produce a credible external control arm regardless of how well it is structured.
Falsification 3: The functional endpoints are so dominated by maturation effects in the 4–7 year target population that no natural history model can reliably separate treatment effect from normal developmental trajectory. Natural history studies indicate that time-function tests are more responsive to treatment effect and more applicable to the 4–7 year old treatment group in DMD clinical studies, but younger patients under seven are more challenging to evaluate due to variability in mood and cooperation. Clinical Trials Arena If this variability is irreducible, the platform's value for early-stage programme design is limited.

ADR-S4: The translational hypothesis — the chain from target to measured endpoint
The chain, stated explicitly
For exon-skipping therapies, the translational chain has five links. Each link is a bet, and the platform's data models each link separately so failures can be localised.
Link 1: Frameshift mutation → absent functional dystrophin
The out-of-frame deletion disrupts the reading frame, producing a truncated, non-functional protein or triggering nonsense-mediated decay. The dystrophin-associated protein complex (DAPC) cannot assemble. The sarcolemma is mechanically vulnerable to contraction-induced damage. This link is established beyond reasonable doubt.
Link 2: Absent dystrophin → membrane damage → CK release → inflammation → fibrosis
Sarcolemmal fragility produces micro-tears during contraction. CK leaks into serum — hence elevated CK as a diagnostic marker. Repeated cycles of damage and incomplete regeneration progressively replace contractile tissue with fibrotic and fatty tissue. This is the mechanism that MRI fat fraction measures. This link is established. CK elevation precedes functional decline by years.
Link 3: Exon skipping → reading frame restoration → truncated but functional dystrophin → partial DAPC assembly → reduced membrane fragility
The ASO induces skipping of the targeted exon, converting an out-of-frame deletion to an in-frame deletion, producing a shorter but partially functional dystrophin — the Becker muscular dystrophy phenotype in miniature. This link is established in principle but the threshold matters: how much dystrophin is enough, and in which tissues, to confer meaningful protection? Del-zota at 5 mg/kg demonstrated 25% of normal dystrophin production — described as some of the best exon-skipping data seen from any drug. BioSpace Whether 25% is sufficient for functional benefit is a central unanswered question.
Link 4: Reduced membrane fragility → attenuated CK → slower fat infiltration → maintained functional trajectory
If link 3 holds, the prediction is: treated patients show declining CK, slowing fat fraction accumulation on MRI, and maintained or improved functional test scores compared to matched natural history controls. In the SAFARI44 Phase 3 trial of del-zota, Phase 1/2 data showed greater than 80% CK reductions from baseline approaching normal in treated individuals, alongside functional stabilisation versus natural history decline over 12–24 months in the open-label extension. Neurology Live This is the strongest current evidence that link 4 holds.
Link 5: Maintained functional trajectory → meaningful clinical outcome → regulatory endpoint
Once the 10-metre walk/run test value exceeds 10 seconds, 100% of DMD patients in natural history data will lose the ability to ambulate within the next two years. Clinical Trials Arena The platform encodes this threshold as a computable rule — a landmark event that transforms a continuous measurement into a binary milestone prediction. This is what makes the data practically useful for trial design: the platform can identify, from baseline functional and biomarker data, which patients are at imminent risk of milestone events, and therefore where a treatment effect would be detectable fastest.
Where the chain is weakest
The weakest link is between dystrophin expression (link 3) and functional benefit (link 4). Efforts should concentrate on understanding the discrepancy between high microdystrophin expression and absent statistically significant functional improvement, whether due to missed therapeutic windows, differences between microdystrophin and full-length dystrophin, or trial design. The Lancet The platform's hypothesis is that better population stratification — particularly by age-at-treatment, baseline functional score, and mutation class — will resolve some of this discrepancy. But it cannot resolve the possibility that microdystrophin itself, regardless of expression level, is functionally inferior to full-length dystrophin in a way that limits the treatment effect ceiling.

ADR-S5: When does a target have enough evidence to move up the regulatory ladder
The decision framework from the programme team's perspective
A DMD programme team makes three distinct regulatory transitions, each requiring a different evidence threshold. The platform is relevant to all three.
Transition 1: Preclinical → IND (Phase 1 entry)
Evidence required: mechanism validation in animal model, safety package, manufacturing consistency, and — for exon-skipping — demonstration that the ASO induces the predicted exon skip and dystrophin restoration in relevant tissue. The platform is not the primary tool here, but it contributes by establishing what exon-skipping level in animal models corresponds to functional improvement in existing clinical data, providing a biological benchmark for the first-in-human dose.
Transition 2: Phase 1/2 → Phase 2/3 (pivotal trial justification)
This is where the platform has maximum leverage. The programme team needs to justify: trial population definition (which mutation subgroup, which age range, which ambulatory status), endpoint selection (which functional test is most sensitive at this disease stage), sample size (what effect size is clinically meaningful and detectable in this population), and — if pursuing accelerated approval — whether dystrophin expression is a credible surrogate.
In the ENDEAVOR study, SRP-9001-treated patients improved 4 points on the NSAA compared with a propensity-weighted external control group over one year. These patients demonstrated divergence from the natural history of DMD over time. CGTlive® The external control arm was constructed from natural history data matched on age, baseline NSAA, and ambulatory status. That matching is exactly what the platform enables at scale. The threshold for moving to pivotal is: a Phase 1/2 signal where the treated trajectory diverges from the matched natural history control by more than the minimum clinically important difference (MCID), defined as approximately 3–4 points on the NSAA, with dystrophin expression as a supportive surrogate.
Transition 3: Accelerated approval → traditional approval (confirmatory requirement)
Elevidys received accelerated approval in 2023 based on dystrophin expression as a surrogate biomarker, then received full approval in 2024 despite unclear efficacy — not meeting primary or secondary outcomes in a Phase 3 trial. PubMed Central The confirmatory pathway requires demonstrating that the surrogate endpoint — dystrophin expression — translates to functional benefit in the confirmatory population. The platform supports this by enabling ongoing monitoring of real-world outcomes in treated patients against matched natural history controls, which is the post-approval evidence generation strategy that Translarna's programme failed to execute convincingly.

ADR-S6: External data integrations and assumptions about them
Decision
The platform integrates four external data sources, each with explicit assumptions that can be violated and must be monitored.
Source 1: TREAT-NMD DMD Global Database
Content: mutation records for over 7,000 patients across 31 countries. Format: HGVS notation inconsistently applied across contributing centres; some records use legacy nomenclature. Assumption: mutation notation can be normalised to canonical HGVS using automated parsing with manual validation of edge cases. Violation condition: if a material fraction of mutations are recorded in non-standard formats that resist automated normalisation, the eligibility classification layer produces errors silently. Mitigation: the platform flags any mutation record that fails HGVS validation rather than silently classifying it.
Source 2: ClinicalTrials.gov API
Content: trial eligibility criteria, endpoints, sponsor, phase, status. Format: free text eligibility criteria that must be parsed to extract structured inclusion/exclusion rules. Assumption: NLP parsing of eligibility criteria produces a structured representation that correctly captures the mutation-amenability rule for each trial. Violation condition: eligibility criteria are frequently ambiguous, using clinical language that does not map directly to mutation classification logic. The platform's NLP layer requires validation against a manually reviewed gold standard set. Currently four FDA-approved antisense oligonucleotides target three different exons, but therapies are mutation-specific and only benefit a subset of patients. PubMed Central The exon target for each trial is often stated in the eligibility criteria — but the reading frame consequence is not, requiring inference from TREAT-NMD mutation data.
Source 3: Published natural history cohorts
Content: longitudinal functional test data from published DMD cohorts (CINRG, NorthStar, TREAT-NMD natural history studies). Format: varied across publications; not all publish individual patient data. Assumption: published summary statistics and individual patient data (where available as supplementary material or via data sharing agreements) are sufficient to parameterise a natural history model stratified by mutation class, age, and steroid status. Violation condition: published natural history data is selectively reported — patients with slower progression are over-represented in published cohorts because they remain in studies longer. This survivorship bias in the natural history model would produce an external control arm that underestimates natural progression speed, making treatment effects appear larger than they are. The platform flags this risk explicitly in every external control arm it produces.
Source 4: UniProt/Ensembl dystrophin gene annotations
Content: exon coordinates, isoform definitions, domain annotations for the dystrophin protein. Assumption: the canonical transcript (NM_004006) and its exon coordinates are stable and correctly annotated. This assumption holds — these are highly curated reference resources with versioned releases. The platform pins to a specific Ensembl release and documents the version in the data lineage.

ADR-S7: Required reproducibility — computational and biological
Decision
The platform distinguishes three reproducibility requirements with different standards: computational reproducibility (highest standard, fully automated), biological measurement reproducibility (bounded by published validation data for each assay), and analytical reproducibility (specified by intended use, not by arbitrary thresholds).
Computational reproducibility
Every transformation in the platform — mutation normalisation, reading frame classification, eligibility mapping, natural history model parameterisation — must be fully deterministic and version-controlled. The same input data must produce the same output on any machine, at any time, using a pinned software environment. This is not aspirational — it is the minimum standard for a regulatory-supporting platform. The reading frame rule is a pure function: given an exon deletion with defined boundaries, the reading frame consequence is computed, not looked up. The output is reproducible by construction.
Biological measurement reproducibility
Each biomarker tier has a published reproducibility profile that constrains what analytical claims the platform can support:
Functional tests: High intra-patient variability. Functional tests are often affected by patient motivation, evaluator engagement, and high intra-patient variability, limiting the possibility to detect relevant changes in patients' trajectories. medRxiv The platform models this variability explicitly — storing test-retest data where available and flagging single-visit measurements as less reliable than longitudinal averages.
Serum biomarkers: Assay-dependent. Cr/Crn by LC-MS/MS has low inter-laboratory variability when using standardised protocols. CK by clinical chemistry has higher inter-laboratory variability and is sensitive to pre-analytic conditions (exercise, posture). The platform stores the assay method as a mandatory field and does not aggregate CK values across different methods without appropriate calibration.
MRI: MR spectroscopy fat fraction and MRI-T2 show high reproducibility with Pearson's R greater than 0.95, but responsiveness to disease progression varies by disease stage and by which muscle group is measured. PubMed Central The platform stores acquisition metadata — vendor, field strength, sequence parameters, muscle group analysed — as mandatory fields. Cross-site MRI comparisons are only valid when acquisition parameters are harmonised, and the platform enforces this constraint explicitly.
Dystrophin expression: The lowest reproducibility of all measured quantities. Method (Western blot vs. capillary Western vs. immunofluorescence), antibody choice, muscle sampled (tibialis anterior vs. vastus lateralis), and biopsy site within the muscle all affect the result. Expression of microdystrophin after gene therapy administration was confirmed by immunofluorescence staining at week 8 post-treatment PubMed Central — but the quantification method and normalisation standard vary across trials. The platform does not attempt to harmonise dystrophin expression values across studies with different methods. It stores them as method-specific measurements and prevents cross-method comparisons unless explicitly requested with appropriate caveats.
Analytical reproducibility — the standard set by intended use
A natural history model used to construct an external control arm for a regulatory submission requires a higher analytical reproducibility standard than a landscape analysis produced for programme planning. The platform distinguishes these use cases explicitly:
Programme planning outputs (which mutation subgroups have the most patients, which trials are currently recruiting, what does the endpoint landscape look like): these require computational reproducibility but not biological measurement harmonisation. They are correct to the precision of the underlying public data.
External control arm construction (a matched cohort used to support a single-arm trial): this requires explicit documentation of the matching variables, the natural history data sources, the exclusion criteria, and the statistical model. The platform generates a data lineage document for every external control arm that records all of these choices, which regulatory agencies will scrutinise.

What Business ADRs Should Capture
The market and competitive positioning decision
Why this disease area? Why now? What does the competitive landscape look like and what assumptions does the platform bet on? This is the ADR everything else implicitly depends on, but it's usually never written down.

Build vs. buy vs. partner decisions
For each major capability (data acquisition, analytics, regulatory submission tooling), what was considered and why was it rejected? These decisions get revisited constantly as the company scales and people forget why the original choice was made.

The data monetization or sharing strategy
Will data products ever be shared externally — with CROs, academic partners, regulators, or commercial partners? This has massive downstream consequences for the governance architecture you've already designed and should be an explicit decision, not an assumption.

Organizational scaling assumptions
The Hub/Spoke model in ADR-3 assumes domains will eventually own their data products. When does that transition happen? What triggers it — headcount, funding round, regulatory milestone? Without this, the Hub team expands indefinitely and the mesh never matures.


ADR-C1: What market problem justifies building a DMD data mesh
The problem the client actually has
A pharma or biotech organisation running a DMD programme faces a data problem that is structurally unlike any other disease area, because DMD therapy eligibility is mutation-specific by design. This is not a data quality problem. It is a data architecture problem: the therapy only works for patients whose mutation falls within a specific exon, reading frame class, and structural category — and no existing clinical data system encodes that relationship in a queryable form.
Currently four FDA-approved antisense oligonucleotides target three different exons, and these therapies are mutation-specific and only benefit a subset of patients. Exon 51 is the most relevant single target, applicable for 10.6% of all mutations and 17.2% of large deletions. Single and double exon-skipping approaches are applicable for 92.8% of large deletions and 90.3% of all mutations. PubMed Central
What this means in data terms: every patient in a trial, in a registry, or in a real-world follow-up dataset has a mutation with a specific exon address, a reading frame consequence, and a therapy eligibility class. Those three facts are not stored together anywhere in a standard clinical data system — they exist in separate sources (molecular diagnostic reports, registry databases, trial eligibility screening records) that are never joined. The data mesh is the join.
The TREAT-NMD DMD Global Database holds genetic data for more than 7,000 DMD mutations. Analysing the type and frequency of patient-specific mutations is described as an invaluable tool for diagnostics, basic scientific research, trial planning, and improved clinical care. PubMed Central The data exists. The problem is that it is not connected to trial data, endpoint data, or therapy access data in a form that produces the answers programme teams actually need.
Why this has become urgent now
The urgency is regulatory, not scientific. Two events in 2024–2025 have made it existential for every DMD programme team.
Event 1: Translarna. Real-world data from patient registries failed to provide conclusive evidence due to methodological limitations and biases. The EMA's Committee for Medicinal Products for Human Use concluded in 2024 that the effectiveness of Translarna could not be confirmed. As a result, in March 2025 the European Commission decided not to renew the conditional marketing authorisation, and Translarna is no longer approved in the EU. PubMed Central This was not a failure of the molecule — it was a failure of the data infrastructure that was supposed to generate post-authorisation real-world evidence. The registry data existed but could not be used convincingly because the mutation classification, patient eligibility, and outcome data were never properly joined.
Event 2: Elevidys Phase III. The Phase III trial of Elevidys did not meet its primary endpoint (North Star Ambulatory Assessment, NSAA) among treated patients compared to placebo. Despite this, mean micro-dystrophin expression at week 12 and key secondary endpoints numerically favoured treatment. Due to safety concerns, Sarepta paused Elevidys shipments for nonambulatory patients in June 2025 after reports of two deaths among nonambulatory recipients. PubMed Central
The endpoint failure and the safety signals are two separate problems, but both are rooted in the same underlying issue: the trial population was not sufficiently stratified by mutation class, ambulatory status, and AAV antibody titre in a way that could cleanly answer the primary question. Choosing the right trial endpoint remains an evolving paradigm in DMD. The key challenge is disease heterogeneity, as subtle changes are heavily dependent on the stage of the disease. Clinical Trials Arena That heterogeneity is computable — if the mutation data, disease stage, functional status, and endpoint trajectory are joined in a single model. Without that join, you get a mixed population that produces inconclusive results.
The client decision
A pharma or biotech organisation with an active DMD programme decides to build a data mesh when they believe that the cost of a trial failure or regulatory rejection attributable to inadequate data infrastructure exceeds the cost of building the infrastructure. Given that a Phase III DMD trial costs $50–200 million, and Translarna's EU withdrawal represents the loss of a commercial product, that calculation is not close.

ADR-C2: Why build it now rather than waiting
The forcing events have already occurred
The client is not deciding whether this problem exists. It already has concrete examples — Translarna and Elevidys — of what happens when it goes unsolved. The question is whether to solve it before the next regulatory checkpoint or after.
In June 2024, Pfizer shared that the pivotal Phase III study of its gene therapy fordadistrogene movaparvovec failed to demonstrate superiority against placebo when evaluated against the NSAA endpoint. In October 2023, Sarepta Therapeutics' Elevidys also failed its Phase III study in the same patient group using NSAA as a primary endpoint. Clinical Trials Arena Two consecutive Phase III failures on the same endpoint in the same disease, both attributed to heterogeneous patient populations and endpoint sensitivity, have made the endpoint-stratification problem visible to every DMD programme team in the industry.
The pipeline timing creates urgency
With the DMD treatment market projected to expand from $2.2 billion in 2023 to $7.4 billion by 2034, multiple companies are advancing next-generation exon skippers, gene therapies, and medicines targeting more than skeletal muscle. Regenxbio expects to submit a BLA in 2026. Avidity is targeting exon 44 — for which there are currently no approved drugs — potentially opening treatment for approximately 6% of DMD patients. BioSpace
Each of these programmes needs, before it reaches pivotal Phase III, to answer: which patients are eligible for my therapy, what does their natural history look like compared to the trial population, and what endpoint will be sensitive enough to detect a treatment effect in a mutation-stratified population? Those questions require the data mesh. The client who builds it now has it ready for pivotal trial design. The client who waits builds it reactively during or after a trial failure.
The regulatory bar is rising, not falling
A new registry for Duchenne and Becker Muscular Dystrophy is being established under FAIR principles — findable, accessible, interoperable, and reusable — specifically to standardise and harmonise care and support evaluation of disease-modifying therapies, with international collaboration for global comparability. clinicaltrials The fact that the field is now explicitly building FAIR-principles registries signals that regulators and patient advocacy groups are raising the bar on data quality and interoperability. Companies that are not already building to this standard will face it as a requirement rather than a choice.

ADR-C3: What the competitive landscape looks like from the client's chair
The client's real alternatives
A client deciding whether to commission a data mesh is not comparing vendors. They are comparing four options:
Option 1: Build internally. Hire a data engineering team and have them integrate TREAT-NMD, ClinicalTrials.gov, and trial data in-house. The problem: TREAT-NMD registries encompass over 13,500 DMD patients across 31 countries, with 94% usage reported for clinical research Academia.edu — but the data requires specialist genomic knowledge to use correctly. An internal data engineering team without DMD domain expertise will build a technically correct schema that is scientifically wrong. The mutation classification alone — exon number, reading frame consequence, therapy eligibility class — requires someone who understands the dystrophin gene structure. Most data engineers do not.
Option 2: Use a CRO data service. Commission IQVIA, Parexel, or a specialist RWE vendor to provide a DMD data product. The problem: these vendors provide general clinical data infrastructure, not disease-specific semantic models. They will give you a correctly formatted dataset with mutation fields — but the reading frame rule will not be embedded as a transformation. The eligibility logic will be in a spreadsheet attached to the report, not in the data layer.
Option 3: Wait for a consortium solution. The FAIR-DMD registry and the D-RSC consortium are building shared infrastructure, but on academic timelines. The CHMP concluded in 2024 that the effectiveness of Translarna could not be confirmed, with real-world data from patient registries failing to provide conclusive evidence due to methodological limitations and biases. PubMed Central Waiting for the field to solve the problem collectively has already cost one company its EU marketing authorisation. The consortium solution will arrive — but too slowly for a company with a pivotal trial scheduled for 2026 or 2027.
Option 4: Commission a scientific data engineer to build it. This is the option your engagement represents. The client gets the semantic model built correctly — mutation classification embedded as a transformation, eligibility rules computable, endpoint trajectory joinable to natural history data — on a timeline that supports their programme milestones. The cost is bounded, the output is inspectable, and the architectural decision records document every scientific assumption that went into the model.
The client chooses option 4 when they have a concrete regulatory milestone within 18–24 months and cannot wait for options 1–3 to deliver.

ADR-C4: What assumptions the client's decision rests on
The bets the client is making when they commission a DMD data mesh
Bet 1: Mutation-to-therapy eligibility is the key stratification variable for their programme.
This holds for every exon-skipping and gene therapy programme. Currently approved exon-skipping drugs target DMD mutations amenable to exons 51, 53, or 45 skipping to produce a truncated dystrophin protein. PubMed Central Any company developing a therapy targeting a specific exon needs to know, from a queryable dataset, how many patients in each registry carry an amenable mutation, what their natural history trajectory looks like, and how they compare to the proposed trial population. If their programme targets a different modality — givinostat, which received FDA approval in March 2024 for ambulant boys aged 6 or over with DMD after a randomised 18-month Phase III study PubMed Central and is applicable to all genetic variants — then mutation stratification is less critical. That client should not be commissioning this specific data mesh.
Bet 2: Natural history data from TREAT-NMD and published cohorts is sufficient to construct an external control arm or to validate trial eligibility criteria.
This is the scientific bet at the heart of the mesh. TREAT-NMD registries encompass over 13,500 DMD patients across 31 countries. Academia.edu If that data, joined to published natural history cohorts and trial metadata, produces a statistically credible external control arm or eligibility validation set, the mesh has clear regulatory utility. If regulators reject external control arms for the specific DMD indication, the mesh's value falls back to programme planning and endpoint selection — still valuable, but less immediately regulatory-grade.
Bet 3: The endpoint problem in DMD is solvable by better stratification, not by finding a fundamentally different endpoint class.
This is the deepest scientific bet. There have been "vogues" of different functional measures over time, where each outcome measure stood under the limelight for being more sensitive to change than its predecessor before being snubbed for the next popular measure. Clinical Trials Arena If the field's conclusion is that all functional endpoints are too variable in heterogeneous populations and the real solution is biomarker endpoints (dystrophin expression level) rather than better-stratified functional endpoints, then a data mesh built around functional endpoint trajectory has limited value. The client should only commission this infrastructure if their programme team believes that better population stratification — which the mesh enables — is a significant part of the answer to the endpoint problem.
Bet 4: Integrating data from multiple open sources produces a product that is more useful than any single source alone.
An updated analysis of exon-skipping applicability using the UMD-DMD database evaluated the theoretical applicability of all single and double exon-skipping strategies across all DMD mutations reported in the database, leveraging phenotypic data for each mutation to provide a more comprehensive and clinically relevant perspective. PubMed Central The UMD-DMD database gives you mutation applicability. TREAT-NMD gives you patient demographics and functional status. ClinicalTrials.gov gives you trial eligibility criteria and endpoint definitions. None of these alone answers the programme team's question: for patients with my target mutation, what does natural history look like, and does the trial population reflect that natural history? The mesh is the join. The client is betting that the join is the valuable thing — not any individual source.
